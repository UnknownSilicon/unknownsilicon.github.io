I've been a bit obsessed with Clarified Milk Punches lately. Every single one I've had has been excellent, even if it's a flavor profile I don't typically enjoy. Everyone I've met says the same thing. They're an incredible way to make complex drinks with a lovely texture, while being broadly enjoyable.  

With upcoming holiday parties, I wanted to make something special. I've always loved the flavor of "spiced orange", so I figured it would be fun to try and make that flavor profile into a milk punch. Spice, orange, citrus, caramel undertones and smooth texture from the milk.

# Clarified Milk Punch?

<!-- Talk about the chemistry of clarifying here -->
Tl;Dr: Milk + tannins + solids + acid -> Smooth, transparent, and bitterness free liquid.

Clarifying cocktails is a neat chemistry trick to remove impurities and bitterness while retaining flavor. There's a handful of ways to clarify, but by far the most interesting way imo is using milk. Specifically, using milk proteins to capture the unwanted compounds. 

Milk contains two primary types of proteins: casein and whey. When casein comes in contact with an acidic environment, the proteins fall out of suspension and form solid clumps throughout the liquid. Due to their porous nature, this mesh of casein is able to trap unpleasent tannins and polyphenols (compounds found in fruits and vegetables that are responsible for their astringency), as well as suspended solids and colors. While the casein falls out of suspension in the acidic environment, the whey remains which provides a silky texture and mouthfeel.

After a round or two of filtering, you're left with a transparent and silky liquid that has all of the complexity of the original cocktail without any unpleasant notes or appearances.

# Infusing

For this spiced orange milk punch, I decided to use infused brandy as the primary flavor. Brandy provides a nice fruity and caramelly base to take on orange and spices.

I used a combination of orange peels, crushed cinnamon sticks, crushed star anise, and cloves. Orange and cloves are the classic "spiced orange" flavor combination, so I focused on those.

To aid in experimentation, I used yet another chemistry (or I suppose physics?) trick: an ultrasonic cleaner.

These devices are typically used to clean jewelry and mechanical parts that are otherwise difficult to clean effectively. By sending ultrasonic waves throughout the water bath, particles stuck to items are shaken off.

{{< img class=center-fig src=/spicedorange/ultrasonic.jpg width=700px alt="A 'DK Sonic' ultrasonic cleaner. There is a basket inside the machine and two knobs on front: One for temperature and one for time. The machine has two LEDs labeled 'Heating' and 'Cleaning', both of which are off." caption="The Ultrasonic Cleaner" >}}

For infusing, two simultaneous effects occur. First, the solution is mixed thoroughly constantly. The waves cause the liquid to vibrate and become turbulent enough to mix. Second, and more importantly, the waves cause micro-cavitations in the solute materials, drastically increasing their surface area and therefore increasing speed of infusion. Ultrasonic waves allow infusing solutions that would otherwise take hours or overnight to occur in about 30 minutes.

Since ultrasonic sound waves can penetrate materials, we can still use this device to infuse liquids inside of a beaker or other container sitting in the water bath.

{{< img class=center-fig src=/spicedorange/infusingbrandy.jpg width=700px alt="A beaker containing the brandy and spices sitting in a bath of water inside of the ultrasonic cleaner. The beaker is on a metal rack.">}}

After all of this, we're left with spiced brandy. It tastes pretty good on it's own (and would have gotten better with a bit of resting time overnight), but using it for the cocktail elevates it to a new level.

# Assembly and Recipe

The main formula here is a spirit, source of sweetness, source of tannins/polyphenols, and a source of acid. I decided to use demerara syrup for sweetness to add deeper molasses notes and both orange juice and black tea for their tannins and polyphenols. The only thing left is acid.

With this combination of flavors, I didn't think more citrus would make sense here. I instead wanted to use malic acid, which is the source of acidity most commonly found in green apples. To get the proper quantity, I acid adjusted the black tea.

Acid adjusting is simply adding an acid solution to another liquid to change the acidity profile. Here, I wanted to make the tea as acidic as lemon juice. The tea already had the correct proportion of liquid for being a source of acid, so this made it easy. I made a 10% malic acid solution and added it to the tea until it tastes as acidic as lemon juice. This doesn't need to be precise.

{{< img class=center-fig src=/spicedorange/mixture.jpg width=700px alt="A beaker with a mixture of the syrup, tea, and orange juice." caption="A beaker with a mixture of the syrup, tea, and orange juice" >}}

And with that, I proportioned ingredients, mixed them together until it was uniform, then poured it over milk to curdle it and turn it into a milk punch.
Mixing this combination occasionally for the first 10 minutes, then letting it settle for the next 20. Finally, I poured it into a coffee filter and let it slowly drain, then poured the liquid back in once more to clarify further.
I'm left with a beautifully clear and orange milk punch. The flavor was great right away, but it got even better when left in the fridge for another 24 hours.

{{< img class=center-fig src=/spicedorange/filtering.jpg width=700px alt="A V60 coffee brewer with a coffee filter in it over a beaker. The curdled milk combination is filtering through the filter." caption="Filtering the Punch" >}}

I'm making another larger batch of this soon, and the only change I'd make is a bit more cinnamon. I've accounted for this in the recipe below.
I will certainly continue performing rapid infusions and clarifying cocktials in the future. These techniques have shown incredibly interesting results.

{{< code title="Recipe" >}}

Infused Brandy:
- 750ml brandy
- Peels of 2-3 oranges ()
- 1-2tsp vanilla extract (to taste)
- ~8g crushed cinnamon sticks (about 3 sticks)
- 2 crushed star anise (about 3 grams)
- ~7g cloves

Combine ingredients in a container. Allow infusing for 24-48 hours, or 40 minutes in an ultrasonic cleaner. If doing a smaller batch, reduce ultrasonic time to 30 minutes.
Strain through mesh strainer.

Spiced Orange Milk Punch:
- 750ml spice infused brandy
- 560ml demerara syrup (280ml if using a rich demerara syrup)
- 180ml orange juice
- 180ml acid adjusted black tea (to taste, 150ml tea, 30ml 10% malic acid)
- 180ml whole milk


1. Acid adjust black tea: Create 10% malic acid solution by weight. Brew black tea, slowly add malic acid until tea is as acidic as lemon juice. Aim for 180ml of acid adjusted tea
2. Combine infused brandy, demerary syrup, orange juice, and acid adjusted black tea. Mix until uniform
3. Slowly pour mixture over milk. Stir whole mixture. Wait 10 minutes
4. After 10 minutes, stir mixture again. Let settle for 20 minutes
5. Pour mixture through coffee filter or other food safe filter. This will take a long time. Be patient and don't agitate the mixture.
6. If desired, pour filtered liquid back over milk curds in the same filter to clarify further. This will take a very long time, but will result in a clearer cocktail
7. Let rest in the fridge for 24 hours before serving
